# Content and marketing guidance for the salon

What our marketing is for.

Every piece exists to fill the appointment book, bring a client back, or sell
a retail product a client genuinely needs. Content that does none of those is
not worth publishing.

What works for a salon.

- Real work from this salon, with the client's permission, is the strongest
  asset we have. Before-and-afters, close-ups of finish and texture.
- Stylist-led content: who they are, what they specialise in, why a client
  should book with them specifically.
- Education: how to maintain a colour between appointments, how long a service
  takes, what to expect at a first visit. Education answers the anxiety that
  stops people booking.
- Local relevance: we serve a catchment area, not the internet. Local search
  terms and local landmarks matter more than broad beauty keywords.

Calls to action.

Every post ends with one clear next step, and that step is almost always to
book or to enquire. Give the actual booking route rather than "DM us" when a
booking link is documented.

What we never do.

No stock imagery presented as our work. No invented reviews or ratings. No
countdown pressure that is not real. No claims that a service is suitable for
everyone. No comparisons to named competitors.
